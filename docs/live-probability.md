# Live probabilities: all tracked sports

This is an experimental, uncalibrated model, not a validated forecast or sportsbook price. It does not change bet grading or place wagers.

Coverage: all seven leagues in the sports repository's config/sports_market_internal_pilot.json supported_sport_markets: NBA, NCAA men's basketball, WNBA, NHL, NFL, NCAA football and MLB; full-game totals, spreads and two-way moneylines. ESPN's fresh exact-game probability takes precedence for moneylines. The coverage contract test compares live-score paths to supportedModelSports so any new score adapter requires an explicit model. Pregame, final, delayed, stale, malformed and unsupported inputs produce no local estimate. Clock-based sports require a valid clock. Baseball requires an unambiguous inning half, outs and base occupancy; its clock is not used. New models require regular/postseason identity. Actual hockey shootouts remain unavailable.

For each team, rate = (current score + prior rate * prior minutes) / (elapsed minutes + prior minutes). Remaining scoring is a compound Poisson distribution with that rate times time remaining. A scoring event has basketball marks 1/2/3 with assumed probabilities .18/.60/.22; hockey marks are single goals. Numerical probability mass is normalized only when the retained mass exceeds .999999. Predictions integrate the joint home/away distributions against the exact saved selection and line. Integer-line equality is a separate push probability.

These are engineering assumptions, not measured current league averages: starting full-game totals NBA 228, NCAA men 144, WNBA 164, NHL 6. Each team's prior is half the total per regulation minute. Prior weight is 12 minutes for basketball, 30 for hockey. No team ratings or bet odds enter this model. An extreme early pace is therefore shrunk toward a neutral prior. Basketball regulation durations are 48/40/40 minutes; NHL 60. Overtime elapsed time is included when deriving observed pace.

Basketball ties use a five-minute overtime scoring distribution conditioned on a non-tie. This approximates repeated overtime and understates totals in multiple-overtime games. Hockey ties resolve with one winner goal, assigning the winner in proportion to the smoothed scoring rates. This approximates overtime/shootout outcomes from regulation; actual shootouts are unavailable. It does not model special bookmaker rules. The independent constant-rate assumption ignores possession, fouls, intentional fouling, penalties, pulled goalies, injuries, lineup strength and tactical changes. Extreme or late-game estimates can be wrong. Predictions are not confidence intervals. No accuracy, edge or calibration claim is made.

Source age uses the scoreboard fetch timestamp; it cannot detect an upstream frozen feed. The existing one-minute scoreboard cache and browser refresh remain. Local predictions use only boards fetched within 90 seconds, guard identity/date/side/line, and cap evaluation at 80 distinct selections per request. Duplicate selections share their calculation. The stacked frontend displays only win/cover chance. Own percentages display whole numbers and <1%/>99% at extremes; ESPN retains its existing precision. Win + lose + push remains available in the API and sums to one within numerical tolerance.

Validation: numerical distribution mass/expectation; over/under complement; selected-side and spread sign; monotonic score/line effects; push separation; clock boundaries and overtime; NHL winning goal; invalid/stale identity rejection; ESPN priority and fallback; browser totals, spread, push and local-source rendering with automatic refresh and failure recovery. These are correctness checks, not historical predictive validation. Historical backtesting with Brier score, log loss and reliability curves by sport/market/time remaining is required before calling the forecasts calibrated.

## Football: football-drive-v1

NFL and NCAA football use 6,000 reproducible simulations of remaining drives. A seed derived from game state (not fetch timestamp or bet line) keeps identical states stable and lets all selections share the same final-score distribution. A bounded 32-state process cache reuses simulations. These are explicit uncalibrated assumptions: typical drive duration 20 + (U1 + U2) * 120 seconds; touchdown probability .25 NFL/.27 NCAA and field goal .15, adjusted by a 30-minute prior with team totals 22 NFL/26 NCAA and current scoring rate, bounded .55–1.65 times baseline. Known current possession is used. When possession is temporarily absent during regulation (for example after an extra point), simulations marginalize over both possible next possessions with equal weight; no possession or field position is invented. A present but invalid team ID fails closed; overtime still requires known possession and drive history. Current field position is resolved from possessionText and verified team abbreviations when available; down/distance alter scoring probabilities, particularly fourth down. Unresolvable field position uses neutral drive assumptions instead of guessing orientation. Very short time reduces scoring likelihood. Confirmed no-timeout kneel-down states can exhaust the clock. Future possessions alternate and use neutral starting field position; turnovers, defensive scores, kickoff returns, detailed timeout strategy and team/player strength are not modeled. Simulated touchdown conversions are .94 for a one-point kick and .48 for a two-point attempt, selected by simplified score logic. This is a coarse drive model, not a play-level forecast.

NFL regular-season overtime is bounded to ten minutes and can end tied (a push for a two-way moneyline). Both sides receive a possession opportunity, subject to clock expiry. After both opportunities a scoring advantage ends the game. Postseason can continue in additional fifteen-minute periods. NCAA overtime uses paired possessions, mandatory two-point conversion attempts in the second overtime, and alternating two-point attempts from the third. Current overtime requires verified summary drive history to identify completed possessions; when that context is unavailable there is no local estimate. Overtime scoring/drive timing remains approximate, especially during conversion attempts.

## Baseball: baseball-state-v1

MLB uses 6,000 reproducible plate-appearance simulations from the current inning, half, outs and three base-occupancy booleans. Required data is never synthesized as empty bases. Between-inning states explicitly normalize to the next half. A bottom ninth or extra half is skipped when the home team already leads. A non-home-run walk-off stops at the winning run; home runs count all scoring runners. Extra innings continue until a winner, with an automatic runner on second only in the regular season. No clock-based extrapolation is used.

Explicit uncalibrated plate-appearance probabilities: out .68, walk .085, single .15, double .045, triple .005, home run .035. Outs can produce a simplified double play (.12 when eligible) or sacrifice fly (.12 when eligible). Singles score a runner from second with probability .6 and can advance a runner from first to third with probability .25; doubles score a runner from first with probability .5. Force advancement on walks is modeled. This ignores batter/pitcher quality, substitutions, park/weather, errors, steals, balls/strikes, bunts and detailed baserunning decisions. A 50-inning/2,000-appearance bound fails closed if a simulation does not finish. Only normal nine-inning regular/postseason MLB games are supported, not exhibitions with special ending rules.

New-model verification includes all seven leagues times three markets, exact-line complements, pushes, repeatability, field position, possession, kneel-downs, NFL ties vs NCAA resolved overtime, postseason overtime, base/out validation, runners/outs effects, skipped bottom halves, walk-offs, extra runners and API enrichment/unchanged grading. These checks establish implementation behavior, not predictive accuracy. Baseball verification outside the MLB season uses labeled fixtures, not fabricated live-game proof.
